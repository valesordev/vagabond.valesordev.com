# Mid Hills — Mojave National Preserve

**Status:** Researched (not yet visited)
**Type:** Established campground + nearby dispersed
**Permit:** None (established campground is first-come, first-served)
**Stay limit:** 14 days
**Elevation:** ~5,600ft

---

## Overview

Mid Hills is the highest accessible camping area in the preserve at ~5,600ft,
sitting in a piñon pine and juniper woodland that feels nothing like the lower
Mojave desert. Temperatures run 15–20°F cooler than the valley floor, making it
viable even in shoulder months (Apr/May, Sep/Oct) when the lower desert is brutal.
The Mid Hills Campground has 26 sites with tables, fire rings, and a vault toilet.
Water is available seasonally (call ahead to confirm). Dispersed camping is possible
on BLM-adjacent land outside the campground boundary.

This is the best bet for comfort camping in the Mojave, especially for longer stays —
the cooler temps reduce power and water consumption significantly.

---

## Access & Road Conditions

**Primary approach:** I-15 N → Cima Rd south → Wild Horse Canyon Rd west to campground
**Alternate approach:** Essex Rd (from I-40) → Black Canyon Rd north → Wild Horse Canyon Rd

- **Cima Rd** is paved; **Wild Horse Canyon Rd** is a maintained dirt road
- Wild Horse Canyon Rd is well-graded and accessible in a stock 4Runner
- Some wash crossings — can be problematic after heavy rain; dry the vast majority of the time
- Cedar Canyon Rd connects Mid Hills area to Hole-in-the-Wall (scenic 11mi dirt loop)
- **Seasonal:** High-elevation terrain; in rare cold snaps (Dec–Feb) light snow
  possible; access usually still fine with 4WD but worth checking conditions

**Coordinates (Mid Hills Campground):** ~35.07°N, 115.46°W

## Site Quality

- **Terrain:** Pine/juniper woodland with rocky outcrops; sites carved into hillside
- **Flat ground:** Campground sites are graded; dispersed areas more variable
- **Shade:** Good to excellent — piñon pines provide actual shade, unlike lower desert
- **Wind:** Can be gusty on exposed ridge — the trees break it at campground sites
- **Views:** Eastern views over the flat desert below are dramatic, especially at dawn
- **Privacy:** Campground sites are reasonably spaced; dispersed camping away from the
  campground gives full privacy
- **Night sky:** Very dark; slightly more light pollution than Cima Dome from distant
  towns but still exceptional by any standard
- **Temps:** Even in Oct–Nov nights can hit the 30s. Pack accordingly.

## Logistics

### Fuel
- **Top off before leaving pavement.** Nearest fuel is Baker (~35mi north) or
  Essex Road area (nothing reliable — don't count on it)
- Fill to full in Barstow before heading into the preserve

### Water
- Mid Hills Campground has a water spigot — **seasonal availability; call ahead:**
  Kelso Visitor Center 760-252-6108
- Do not rely on campground water; carry full load (7gal minimum) and treat it as a bonus
- In winter, spigot is often shut off to prevent freezing

### Power
The tree cover at Mid Hills will reduce solar yield compared to open desert. Expect
~150–220 Wh/day from the two panels rather than the typical 240–300 Wh. Account
for this if planning multi-day Starlink work sessions — the Jackery 2000 provides
buffer but monitor more carefully.

### Starlink
Moderate suitability — tree canopy can interrupt signal. Positioning the dish in
a clearing away from the 4Runner may be necessary. Performance should be workable
with some positioning effort.

### Bail-Out
- North: Wild Horse Canyon Rd → Cima Rd → I-15 → Baker (~45min)
- South: Cedar Canyon Rd → Essex Rd → I-40 → Needles or west (~40min to I-40)

## Notes & Alerts

- The Mid Hills to Hole-in-the-Wall hiking trail (~8mi one-way) is excellent;
  consider parking a bike at one end for a point-to-point
- Cedar Canyon Road is one of the more scenic drives in the preserve — good for an
  afternoon exploration from camp
- Campground fills on holiday weekends (rare for Mojave, but happens in Oct–Nov)
- Piñon pine nuts are edible and often present in fall — just noting it
