# Hole-in-the-Wall — Mojave National Preserve

**Status:** Researched (not yet visited)
**Type:** Established campground + limited dispersed nearby
**Permit:** None (first-come, first-served)
**Stay limit:** 14 days
**Elevation:** ~4,400ft

---

## Overview

Hole-in-the-Wall is the most developed camping area in the preserve — it has an NPS
visitor contact station, potable water, flush toilets (rare in the Mojave backcountry),
and good paved road access. The namesake geology is a volcanic rhyolite formation with
narrow slot-canyon passages navigated via iron rings drilled into the rock. It's
genuinely impressive and unlike anything else in the preserve.

Two campgrounds here: **Hole-in-the-Wall** (35 sites, RV-accessible) and
**Black Canyon Group** nearby. It's the most "civilized" base camp in the preserve —
useful as a first trip to build familiarity before committing to more remote spots.
Dispersed camping opportunities are limited immediately around the campground due
to the ¼-mile rule from paved roads; head north toward Mid Hills for better dispersed options.

---

## Access & Road Conditions

**Primary approach:** I-40 west → Essex Rd north → Black Canyon Rd north (~20mi from I-40)

- **Essex Rd** and **Black Canyon Rd** are both paved all the way to the campground
- One of the easiest accesses in the preserve — fine in any vehicle
- Also accessible from the north via Cedar Canyon Rd from Mid Hills (dirt)
- **Seasonal:** Paved roads here are maintained; rarely impassable. After heavy
  rain the Cedar Canyon Rd connector may be muddy.

**Coordinates (Hole-in-the-Wall Campground):** ~34.99°N, 115.40°W

---

## Site Quality

- **Terrain:** Rocky volcanic landscape; campsites carved into naturally sheltered pockets
- **Flat ground:** Campground sites are graded and spacious
- **Shade:** Limited — some site shade from rock formations, minimal tree canopy
- **Wind:** Rock formations provide natural wind breaks; generally more sheltered than open desert
- **Views:** Dramatic volcanic rock formations on all sides; not the panoramic views
  of Cima Dome but more intimate and geological
- **Privacy:** Campground sites are reasonably private given the rocky terrain — rock
  walls naturally separate sites
- **Night sky:** Good, though slightly more affected by I-40 corridor light dome than Cima or Mid Hills

## Logistics

### Fuel
- **Essex** (off I-40) — no services. Fill up before taking Essex Rd north.
- Needles (~60mi east on I-40) or Barstow/Ludlow to the west
- **Best approach:** Fill in Barstow (I-15 to I-40) or at a Needles station depending
  on approach direction. Do not arrive at Essex Rd on less than a half tank.

### Water
- **Hole-in-the-Wall Campground has potable water** — this is the best water
  access in the preserve
- Still carry your full load as a baseline; treat campground water as a resupply opportunity
- Confirm availability seasonally: 760-252-6108

### Starlink
Good geometry — relatively open sky above the rock formations. Some obstruction
from the volcanic walls to the south and west depending on site position.
Scout the site after arrival for best dish placement.

### Bail-Out
- South: Black Canyon Rd → Essex Rd → I-40 (~20min to interstate)
- I-40 east to Needles (hospital, fuel): ~60mi
- I-40 west toward Barstow/Victor Valley: ~90mi

This is the easiest bail-out in the preserve thanks to the paved access.

---

## Ring Loop Trail (Geology)

The slot canyon trail through the volcanic rings is the main activity. It's short
(~1mi loop) but requires pulling yourself through narrow passages using iron rings
— not difficult but note it for the cat logistics. The cat stays in the cab zone
during this activity. Worth doing on arrival day as a stretch after the drive.

## Notes & Alerts

- This is the most crowded campground in the preserve, though still quiet by most
  standards. Fills on peak fall weekends.
- Best used as a base camp to access Cedar Canyon Rd (to Mid Hills) and the
  broader southeast preserve
- The Banshee Canyon dispersed area is ~2mi north and avoids the campground crowd
  while still having reasonable access — worth researching as a dispersed alternative
