# Mojave National Preserve

**Status:** Researched
**Drive from SD:** ~3.5–4hr via I-15 N (exit at Cima Rd or Kelbaker Rd depending on destination)
**Land management:** National Park Service
**Permit:** None required for dispersed camping
**Stay limit:** 14 days; must relocate 25 miles after limit

---

## Season & Conditions

| Month | Viability | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| Oct–Nov | Excellent | Cooling off, uncrowded, Joshua trees still green |
| Dec–Feb | Good | Cold nights (20s possible at elevation), clear skies, minimal crowds |
| Mar–Apr | Good | Wildflower potential; temps rising toward end of April |
| May–Sep | Avoid | 100–115°F+ common; monsoon flash flood risk Jul–Sep |

The preserve sits at varying elevations — Mid Hills (~5,600ft) runs 15–20°F cooler
than the lower desert flats. In deep winter, light snow is possible at Mid Hills
and Providence Mountains even when lower areas are mild.

---

## Dispersed Camping Rules

- No dispersed camping within **¼ mile** of any water source, paved road, or developed campsite
- Stay limit: 14 consecutive days; must move at least 25 miles after
- Pack out all waste — no trash facilities in backcountry
- Ground fires: check current restrictions at nps.gov/moja before every trip; often prohibited May–Oct
- Camp on previously disturbed surfaces where possible; avoid cryptobiotic soil crust
- No camping in wash bottoms during or after rain (flash flood risk)

## Regional Logistics

### Fuel
The preserve has no fuel. Stock up before entering.

| Station | Location | Notes |
|---------|----------|-------|
| Baker | I-15 at Kelbaker Rd (west entry) | Last reliable stop westbound on I-15; often expensive |
| Barstow | ~45min west of Baker on I-15 | Better prices; good place to top off before heading out |
| Needles | I-40 / US-95 (east entry) | Option for eastern approach; far from most camp areas |
| Amboy | Historic Route 66, east of 29 Palms | Roy's Motel Cafe — fuel available but verify hours before relying on it |

Plan: **top off in Barstow** (leaving SD via I-15), and carry a mental reserve — most
camp areas are 20–40 miles of dirt from the nearest paved road.

### Water
**There is no reliable water in the Mojave backcountry.** Treat this as non-negotiable.

- Carry **minimum 7 gallons** for solo trips; more for trips >48hr or in mild heat
- Hole-in-the-Wall Campground has a water spigot (verify seasonal availability; sometimes off in winter)
- Mid Hills Campground has water (seasonal — call ahead: 760-252-6100)
- No filtering opportunities in backcountry; all water must be carried in
- 2× 5-gallon containers in current kit = 10 gallons total. Adequate for 3–4 days solo.

### Cell Service
Minimal throughout. Verizon and AT&T get occasional signal near Kelso Depot and
along Kelbaker Road. Essentially dead in most backcountry areas.

**Starlink is essential for any remote work or emergency communication.**
Budget ~300–450 Wh/day for full-day Starlink use. The preserve's remoteness and
low horizon obstructions make Starlink perform well here.

### Emergency Bail-Out Routes
- **West:** Kelbaker Rd → I-15 → Baker (nearest town, ~30min from Kelso)
- **North:** Cima Rd → I-15 → Baker
- **East:** Essex Rd → I-40 → Needles (further, ~1hr from eastern areas)
- **South:** Amboy Rd → 29 Palms Hwy → 29 Palms (for southern approaches)

Nearest hospital: **Desert Valley Hospital, Victorville** (~1.5hr from Kelso via I-15).
Cell service to call 911 is unreliable — satellite communicator is on the build
backlog but should be considered essential for solo Mojave trips.

## Road Notes — General

Most preserve roads are accessible in a stock 5th gen 4Runner. A few specifics:
- **Paved:** Kelbaker Rd, Cima Rd, Essex Rd, Kelso-Cima Rd, Black Canyon Rd (to Hole-in-the-Wall)
- **Good dirt (maintained):** Mid Hills Rd, Wild Horse Canyon Rd, Cedar Canyon Rd
- **Variable / washboard:** Most backcountry spurs and dispersed camping access roads
- Airing down to ~30 psi improves comfort and traction on washboard and soft sand
- After rain, dirt roads can become muddy or develop deeper ruts — check conditions

## Wildlife

- **Mojave rattlesnake** — present April–October; watch around rocks and brush
- **Desert tortoise** — protected; do not disturb; common in lower desert
- **Coyotes** — everywhere; secure food
- **Sidewinder rattlesnake** — sandy areas; less common than Mojave rattler
- **Scorpions** — always shake out shoes; check sleeping area
