# Cima Dome — Mojave National Preserve

**Status:** Researched (not yet visited)
**Type:** Dispersed camping, BLM/NPS
**Permit:** None
**Stay limit:** 14 days

---

## Overview

Cima Dome is a broad, syenite granite formation rising gently to about 5,745ft —
one of the largest near-symmetrical domes in the US. It's blanketed by one of the
densest and most pristine Joshua tree forests in the world, notably different in
character from Joshua Tree NP — the trees are taller and denser here. Wide open
sky, minimal light pollution, no crowds. The area feels genuinely remote despite
being accessible from a paved road.

This is a top dispersed camping candidate for the Mojave — flat ground, excellent
Starlink sightlines, good all-season access via Cima Rd, and far enough from any
developed campground to feel isolated.

---

## Access & Road Conditions

**Primary approach:** I-15 N → Cima Rd exit → south on Cima Rd (~14mi from I-15)

- **Cima Rd** is paved all the way to the Cima post office settlement
- Dispersed camping requires heading onto dirt tracks off Cima Rd or Kelbaker Rd
- Dirt roads in this area are generally well-graded and passable in a stock 4Runner
- No major obstacles; sandy in places but not deep sand
- Airing down to ~30 psi recommended for washboard sections
- **Seasonal note:** Cima Rd occasionally closes briefly after heavy rain; check
  conditions. The dome area drains well and mud is rarely a prolonged issue.

**Coordinates reference (Cima Rd / Mojave Rd intersection area):** ~35.2°N, 115.5°W

---

## Site Quality

- **Terrain:** Gently rolling granite dome with sandy flats between Joshua trees
- **Flat ground:** Generally good; avoid low spots (water collects)
- **Shade:** Partial, from Joshua trees — better than open desert but not full cover
- **Wind:** Can be exposed on the dome; Joshua trees provide some break
- **Views:** Wide open 360° desert panoramas; San Bernardino Mountains visible to SW on clear days
- **Privacy:** Excellent — dispersed sites feel isolated even when others are nearby
- **Night sky:** Outstanding. Cima is one of the darkest accessible spots in SoCal.
- **Cell/radio:** Essentially zero. Verizon may get 1 bar near Cima Rd near I-15.

## Logistics

### Fuel
- **Top off in Barstow** before heading out (takes ~45min to reach Cima Rd from Barstow)
- Baker (I-15 exit, ~20mi from Cima Rd) has fuel but prices are high
- Do not count on returning to Baker mid-trip; fill to full before leaving pavement

### Water
- **No water on site.** Carry full load (7gal minimum, 10gal with both containers).
- Nearest water: Hole-in-the-Wall Campground (~30mi south) — seasonal, call ahead
- Nearest town water: Baker (~20mi north on I-15)

### Starlink
Very favorable geometry — flat horizon in most directions, no canyon walls.
Budget 300–450 Wh/day for full work-from-camp day. The Jackery 2000 handles
overnight fridge + morning Starlink load comfortably with morning solar.

### Bail-Out
- North to Baker via Cima Rd + I-15: ~25min
- West to Barstow via I-15: ~50min from Cima Rd
- No other services in any direction; Baker is the realistic emergency destination

## Notes & Alerts

- Peak wildflower years bring more visitors in March — still not crowded by normal standards
- Joshua tree branches are brittle; don't camp directly under heavy deadfall risk
- If targeting astrophotography, new moon windows in Oct–Dec are exceptional here
- The Mojave Road (historic 4WD route) passes through the broader area — interesting
  to explore but not a camping destination itself
